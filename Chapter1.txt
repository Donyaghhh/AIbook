The rain had been falling since dawn, a soft, steady whisper that turned the forest into a single breathing thing.
Elara pulled her hood tighter and stepped off the trail, drawn by the smell of wet moss and something older beneath it.

The pines stood like patient sentinels, their bark dark as ink, water tracing silver lines down every trunk.
Somewhere above, the canopy swallowed the sky entirely, and the only light came filtered and green, as if she were walking on the floor of a drowned cathedral.

She found the clearing by accident—or perhaps the forest had decided to show it to her.
A ring of stones, half-sunk in the loam, and in the center a pool so still that the raindrops made perfect circles, one after another, like something counting.

An old woman sat on one of the stones, unbothered by the rain, her gray hair plastered to her cheeks.

"You're late," she said.

Elara had never met her before. She was certain of it.

"Late for what?"

The woman smiled, and the rain seemed to pause—just for a heartbeat, just long enough for Elara to notice.

"For the part where you stop running," she said. "The forest doesn't mind. It's been raining here for a hundred years. It can wait a little longer."

Elara sat down on the wet stone beside her. She didn't ask how the woman knew. In a forest that kept its own time, some questions simply dissolved, like sugar in rain.

They watched the pool together, and the circles kept counting, and neither of them said another word.
